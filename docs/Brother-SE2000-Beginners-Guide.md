# Brother SE2000 Beginner's Guide: Henderson Clover Patch

2026-11-20 · Joshua Henderson

> Printable version: [Brother-SE2000-Beginners-Guide.pdf](Brother-SE2000-Beginners-Guide.pdf)

## What you'll make

Your first SE2000 project is the Henderson patch: "Henderson" over "est. 2007", with a small four-leaf clover on each side of the year. It's about 4.2" x 1.3" and fits the 5" x 7" hoop that comes with the machine.

- **The file is `henderson-est-2007-clover.pes`.** PES is Brother's own format, so the machine shows the real thread colors and color names on screen, unlike the DST file made for the Poolin.
- **Work through it together.** One of you reads a step aloud, the other does it, then swap.
- **Do, then check.** Every step ends with a **Check** so you know it worked before moving on, and some have a **Try it** for hands-on practice.
- **Practice on scrap first.** Stitch your first one or two on scrap cotton before using a real shirt or towel.
- **Trust your screen.** Brother menus can differ slightly by software version. If a button name here doesn't match, look for the closest match on your screen or in the printed manual.

## Meet the SE2000

The SE2000 is two machines in one: a regular **sewing machine**, and an **embroidery machine** once you slide the embroidery unit onto the left side. It has a 5" x 7" embroidery area, a color touchscreen, 193 built-in designs, 13 built-in fonts, and wireless transfer through Brother's Artspira app.

Stand in front of it together and find each part. Touch it, open it, close it.

| Part | Where it is | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spool pin and spool cap** | Top of the machine | Holds the top thread. The cap stops the spool from wobbling. |
| **Thread guides** | Along the top and front, numbered | Lead the top thread to the needle in a set path. Follow the numbers printed on the machine. |
| **Take-up lever** | Front, inside the threading channel | Pulls up slack after every stitch. Missing it causes thread nests. |
| **Needle position button** | Front panel | Raises or lowers the needle. Press it before threading so the needle and take-up lever are at the top. |
| **Presser foot lifter button** | Front panel | Raises and lowers the foot. Up to thread, down to stitch. |
| **Presser foot lever** | Behind the needle area | Raises and lowers the foot by hand. |
| **Thread cutter button** | Front panel | Trims both threads. |
| **Start/Stop button** | Front panel | Starts and pauses stitching. Your emergency stop. Green means ready, red means not ready. |
| **Needle threader lever** | Left side, above the needle | Pulls the top thread through the needle eye for you. |
| **Embroidery foot "UH" (with LED pointer)** | Accessory bag | The foot used for embroidery. It replaces the sewing foot and plugs into a jack on the back left. |
| **Lock key** | Touchscreen | Locks the buttons so the machine can't start while your hands are near the needle. Use it every time you thread, change the bobbin, or swap feet. |
| **Bobbin cover and latch** | In front of the needle | Covers the drop-in bobbin. Slide the latch right to open it. |
| **Bobbin winder** | Top right | Fills an empty bobbin from a spool. |
| **Handwheel** | Right side | Turns the needle by hand. Always turn it **toward you**. |
| **Touchscreen** | Front right | Choose, edit, and position designs, and see the color order. |
| **USB port** | Right side | Load design files from a USB flash drive. |
| **Main power switch** | Right side | On/off. |
| **Flat bed attachment** | Left side, in sewing mode | Extension table with an accessory compartment. Slides off to make room for the embroidery unit. |
| **Embroidery unit** | Separate box; slides onto the left side | The carriage on top moves the hoop under the needle. A release lever underneath lets you remove it. |
| **Frame holder and frame-securing lever** | On the embroidery unit's carriage | Grips the hoop. Push the lever down to lock the hoop in. |
| **Embroidery frame (hoop)** | Accessory box | 5" x 7" (130 x 180 mm). Inner and outer frames with an adjustment screw, plus a clear grid sheet for centering. |

### Different from the Poolin

- **It switches between sewing and embroidery.** You attach the embroidery unit and swap the presser foot to embroidery foot "UH" before you embroider.
- **It reads PES files (and DST, PHC, PEN).** PES carries thread colors, so the screen shows the colors you'll actually use.
- **Smaller area.** 5" x 7" instead of 7.9" x 15". The Henderson patch fits easily.

**Try it:** with the machine off, turn the handwheel toward you and watch the needle and take-up lever move up and down together.

## Supplies checklist

Gather these before Step 1. Most came in the SE2000 box.

- [ ] **Top thread, three colors,** 40-weight polyester or rayon embroidery thread. The file is set up for Brother's **Emerald Green** ("Henderson"), **Leaf Green** (clovers), and **Brass** ("est. 2007"). Any similar dark green, light green, and gold work. For a one-color patch, one dark green is fine.
- [ ] **Bobbin with white embroidery bobbin thread** (60-weight; a spool comes in the box). Use the bobbins that came with the machine.
- [ ] **Needle, size 75/11.** Brother recommends 75/11 for embroidery. Put in a fresh one.
- [ ] **Embroidery foot "UH"** from the accessory bag, plus the disc-shaped screwdriver.
- [ ] **Embroidery unit** (separate box) and the **5" x 7" embroidery frame** with its clear grid sheet.
- [ ] **Stabilizer:** medium-weight cut-away or tear-away, cut at least 1" bigger than the frame on every side.
- [ ] **Practice fabric:** plain woven cotton, about 10" x 12". Not stretchy knit for the first try.
- [ ] **USB flash drive,** FAT32, 32 GB or smaller, with `henderson-est-2007-clover.pes` copied onto it.
- [ ] **Small curved snips and tweezers.**
- [ ] **Washable fabric marker or chalk.**

**The file:** download [henderson-est-2007-clover.pes](https://github.com/gabulldogsfan10/embroidery-machine/raw/claude/exciting-thompson-23478r/patterns/henderson-est-2007-clover.pes) from your Embroidery-Machine repository.

## Step 1: Set up and attach the embroidery unit

Goal: machine on a sturdy table with the embroidery unit attached and room for the hoop to move. About 15 minutes.

1. **Pick the spot.** A solid table with about 12" of clear space to the left of and behind the machine. The carriage and hoop move during stitching and can hit anything nearby.
2. **Unpack.** Remove all tape, foam, and cardboard, including anything around the needle and under the embroidery unit's carriage.
3. **Turn the machine off.** Brother says the power must be off before attaching or removing the embroidery unit.
4. **Slide off the flat bed attachment** (the extension table on the left with the accessory compartment). Pull it straight off to the left and set it aside.
5. **Slide the embroidery unit on.** Line up its connector with the connection port on the left side of the machine and push it straight in until it seats firmly.
6. **Plug in and power on.** Keep your hands and anything else clear: the carriage may move to its starting position.
7. **Look at the screen.** You should see the embroidery screen with design categories, fonts, and the memory/USB icons.

**Check:** the unit sits flush against the machine and doesn't wiggle, and the screen shows embroidery options.

**Removing it later (to sew):** power off, pull the **release lever under the embroidery unit**, and slide the unit to the left. Then put the flat bed attachment back.

## Step 2: Put on the embroidery foot "UH"

Goal: the sewing foot and its holder off, embroidery foot "UH" screwed on tight and plugged in. The sewing foot can't be used for embroidery: the needle can hit it.

1. **Raise the needle** with the needle position button.
2. **Touch the lock key** on the screen. If it asks "OK to automatically lower the presser foot?", touch **OK**.
3. **Raise the presser foot lever.**
4. **Remove the sewing foot and holder.** Loosen the presser foot holder screw with the included screwdriver and take the holder (with its foot) off. Keep them in the accessory compartment.
5. **Position the embroidery foot.** Hold it from the right and line up its notch with the presser foot holder screw.
6. **Tighten the screw firmly** with the disc-shaped screwdriver while holding the foot in place with your right hand.
7. **Plug in the LED cord.** Insert the foot's connector into the jack on the back left of the machine.
8. **Put in a fresh 75/11 needle** if you haven't: loosen the needle clamp screw, push the new needle all the way up with the **flat side toward the back**, and tighten.
9. **Touch the unlock key.**

**Check:** wiggle the foot gently. It shouldn't move. Turn the handwheel toward you one full turn: the needle should go down through the foot's opening without touching it.

**Try it:** later, in the embroidery screen, touch the **needle point check key** (the presser foot icon). The foot's LED shows where the needle will land.

## Step 3: Wind and load the bobbin

Goal: a bobbin full of white 60-weight bobbin thread, seated in the bobbin area with its thread in the tension spring. Brother says to wind and insert the bobbin **before** attaching the hoop.

### Wind the bobbin

Follow the bobbin-winding diagram printed on top of the machine; the steps are roughly:

1. Put the white bobbin thread on the spool pin with the spool cap.
2. Lead the thread through the bobbin winding thread guide and around the pretension disk as the diagram shows.
3. Wrap the end clockwise around an empty bobbin 5 or 6 times.
4. Put the bobbin on the bobbin winder shaft and **push the shaft to the right**.
5. Press **Start/Stop**. The light turns orange while winding. It stops when full (or press Start/Stop to stop).
6. Cut the thread, **push the shaft back to the left**, and take off the bobbin.

**Watch out:** while the winder shaft is pushed right, the needle won't stitch. If the motor runs but the needle doesn't move, check that the shaft is back to the left.

### Load the bobbin

1. **Raise the needle** with the needle position button and **touch the lock key**. Raise the presser foot.
2. **Slide the bobbin cover latch to the right** and lift off the cover.
3. **Drop the bobbin in so the thread unwinds to the left** (counter-clockwise when you look down).
4. **Hold the bobbin down** with one finger and guide the thread around the tab, through the slit in the needle plate, and into the tension spring.
5. **Cut the thread** with the cutter by the slit.
6. **Put the cover back:** tab end first, then press the right side down.
7. **Touch the unlock key.**

**Check:** before closing the cover, pull the thread gently: the bobbin should turn smoothly with light resistance. If it spins freely, the thread missed the tension spring; redo step 4.

Use the bobbins and the green-marked bobbin case that came on the machine. Brother notes that bobbins not made for this machine can cause thread breaks.

## Step 4: Thread the top thread

Goal: Emerald Green thread running through every guide and the needle eye. You'll repeat this at every color change, so each of you should do it once now.

The golden rule: **presser foot UP while threading.** With the foot up, the tension discs open so the thread drops between them. Threaded with the foot down, the thread skips the tension and you get a tangle under the fabric.

1. **Raise the needle** with the needle position button (press once or twice until the needle is all the way up).
2. **Touch the lock key** and **raise the presser foot.**
3. **Put the spool on the spool pin** so the thread comes off the **front bottom** of the spool. Push the spool cap on snugly.
4. **Follow the numbered path** printed on the machine: through the thread guide cover, under the thread guide plate, down the right channel, around the bottom, and up the left channel.
5. **Through the take-up lever** at the top of the left channel, then back down the left channel.
6. **Behind the needle bar thread guide** just above the needle.
7. **Use the needle threader:**

   1. Pass the thread through the threader's guide disk marked **"7"**.
   2. Lower the presser foot and cut the thread with the cutter on the left.
   3. Pull the **needle threader lever** down until it clicks, then let it return slowly.
   4. Pull the loop of thread through the needle eye.
8. **Raise the presser foot** and pull about 4" of thread under the foot toward the back.
9. **Touch the unlock key.**

**Check:** lower the presser foot and pull the thread near the needle: firm, even resistance. Raise the foot and pull again: it slides easily. That difference means the thread is in the tension discs. No difference? Re-thread from step 1.

**Hand vs machine:** Brother recommends 40-weight rayon or polyester embroidery thread on top. Don't use thick (20-weight or heavier) thread.

## Step 5: Hoop the fabric

Goal: fabric and stabilizer drum-tight in the 5" x 7" frame, centered on your mark. Your hand-hooping skills apply directly here.

1. **Mark the center.** Fold the fabric in quarters and finger-press, or draw a small cross with a washable marker or chalk.
2. **Take the frame apart.** Loosen the frame adjustment screw and lift out the inner frame.
3. **Stack the layers:** outer frame on the table, stabilizer on it, fabric on top, right side up.
4. **Center with the grid sheet.** Snap the clear embroidery sheet into the inner frame and line its center lines up with your cross.
5. **Press the inner frame in,** lining up its marks with the outer frame's marks. Remove the grid sheet.
6. **Tighten a little, then smooth.** Snug the screw slightly, gently pull the fabric edges until it's taut and flat, check that the inner and outer frames sit evenly, then tighten the screw fully.
7. **Orient it for the machine.** The design's top should point away from you (toward the back of the machine) once the frame is attached; the frame's arrow marks the top.

**Check:** tap the fabric: it should sound like a drum and barely give under a fingertip. The fabric must not slide between the frames.

**Hand vs machine:** on a hand hoop you re-tighten as you go; here you can't touch it once stitching starts. A loose hoop is the top cause of wavy letters and outlines that don't line up.

## Step 6: Load the design and attach the hoop

Goal: the Henderson clover design on screen, centered, with the hoop locked onto the carriage.

![Henderson clover design in Brother thread colors](../patterns/henderson-est-2007-clover-brother.png)

| Detail | Value |
| --- | --- |
| File | `henderson-est-2007-clover.pes` |
| Size | 106 x 32 mm (about 4.2" x 1.3") |
| Stitches | about 6,400 |
| Colors, in order | 1. Emerald Green ("Henderson"), 2. Leaf Green (both clovers), 3. Brass ("est. 2007") |
| Frame | 5" x 7" (130 x 180 mm) |

### Load the file

1. **Copy the .pes file to a USB flash drive** (FAT32, 32 GB or smaller). Put it at the top level, not in a folder. Keep file names to letters, numbers, `-` and `_`.
2. **Plug the drive into the USB port** on the right side of the machine.
3. On the embroidery screen, touch the **memory/retrieve icon**, then the **USB icon**. (If the file is in a folder, touch the folder.)
4. **Touch the design's thumbnail**, then **Set**. If it asks for a frame, choose the **5" x 7" (130 x 180 mm)** frame.
5. On the **Edit** screen, leave the size alone for the first run and use the **center key** if it isn't already centered. Then touch **Embroidery**.
6. **Optional:** save it to the machine's memory so you don't need the USB stick next time.

### Attach the hoop

1. **Raise the presser foot** with the presser foot lifter button.
2. **Slide the frame into the frame holder,** with the frame guide against the right edge of the holder and the frame's arrow lined up with the holder's mark.
3. **Push the frame-securing lever down** until it's level with the frame.

**Check:** the screen shows three colors in the color order list: Emerald Green, Leaf Green, Brass. The design sits inside the frame outline on screen, and the hoop doesn't wiggle in the holder.

**Try it:** touch the **needle point check key** (presser foot icon). The LED on the foot shows where the first stitch will land. Compare it to your center mark. Use the move arrows on the Layout screen to nudge the design if needed.

## Step 7: Stitch it

Goal: a finished stitch-out with three colors. Stay at the machine the whole time on your first run.

1. **Check the thread:** Emerald Green on top (Step 4), white in the bobbin (Step 3).
2. **Clear the area** around the carriage and hoop. Nothing should be within reach of the moving hoop.
3. **Lower the presser foot.** The Start/Stop light should be green. Red means something isn't ready (foot up, hoop not seated, or a message on screen).
4. **Hold the thread tail** lightly to the side for the first few stitches.
5. **Press Start/Stop.** Watch the first 30 seconds: stitches should lie flat with no loops and no white showing on top. If you hear rattling or see loops, press Start/Stop right away.
6. **Snip the starting tail** after a few stitches if the machine pauses, so it doesn't get stitched over.
7. **Color change.** After "Henderson", the machine trims the thread, stops, and raises the foot. The color list moves Leaf Green to the top.

   1. Cut the Emerald Green thread near the spool and pull it out **from the needle end** (pulling from the spool end drags it backward through the tension).
   2. Thread Leaf Green (Step 4).
   3. Press Start/Stop. Both clovers stitch in this color.
8. **Repeat for Brass** ("est. 2007").
9. **Finish.** The screen shows **Finished embroidering**.

**Check:** the screen's counter shows colors 3/3 and the full stitch count. Turn the hoop over: you should see mostly white bobbin thread with a thin edge of color around each letter. That's a balanced stitch.

**Good to know:** the SE2000 trims at the end of each color ("End Color Trim") and trims jumps between letters ("Jump Stitch Trim"), both on by default. The jump-trim length is in Settings (5–50 mm); leave the default for now. If you stop for a while and the machine goes into power-saving mode, Brother notes it restarts from the beginning, so don't walk away mid-design.

## Step 8: Finish the patch

Goal: a clean patch with no loose threads and the stabilizer trimmed.

1. **Raise the presser foot,** lift the frame-securing lever, and pull the hoop toward you to remove it.
2. **Loosen the frame screw** and push the inner frame out from behind. Don't pull on the stitched area.
3. **Trim any thread tails** on the front close to the fabric with curved snips.
4. **Trim the stabilizer.** Cut-away: trim to about 1/4" around the design, rounding the corners. Tear-away: hold the stitches with your thumb and tear away from them a little at a time.
5. **Press from the back** with a medium iron on a towel so you don't flatten the stitches.

**Try it:** stitch it again on a real item, like a tote bag, towel, or shirt. For towels, add a water-soluble topper on top so stitches don't sink into the loops. For T-shirts, use cut-away stabilizer.

**Back to sewing:** power off, pull the release lever under the embroidery unit and slide it off, put the flat bed attachment back, and swap foot "UH" for the sewing foot and its holder.

## Troubleshooting

When something goes wrong, press Start/Stop, then work down this order: **re-thread the top thread (foot up) → re-seat the bobbin → change the needle → re-hoop.**

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tangled thread under the fabric, rattling noise | Top thread threaded wrong (often with the foot down) | Cut the tangle free, clean the bobbin area, re-thread the top thread with the foot up, reload the bobbin |
| Top thread too tight, white showing on top | Bobbin threaded wrong | Reload the bobbin so the thread goes through the tension spring |
| Top thread breaks | Wrong or loose spool cap, missed a guide, knots in thread, wrong needle | Re-thread, use the right spool cap, fresh 75/11 needle |
| Bobbin thread breaks | Bobbin set or wound wrong, or not a Brother bobbin for this machine | Re-wind and reload with an included bobbin |
| Motor runs but needle doesn't move | Bobbin winder shaft still pushed right | Push the shaft back to the left |
| Start/Stop light is red | Presser foot up, hoop not locked, or a message on screen | Lower the foot, re-seat the hoop, read the message |
| Fabric puckers | Not enough stabilizer, or loose hoop | Add a stabilizer layer and re-hoop drum-tight |
| Carriage or hoop bumps something | Object too close | Clear 12" around the machine |

### Care

- Brush lint from the bobbin area every few projects (power off first).
- Change the needle every 8–10 hours of stitching, or when thread starts shredding.
- Lock the keys whenever your fingers go near the needle.
